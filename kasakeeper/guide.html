KasaKeeper

User guide · the maintenance brain for your house

KasaKeeper watches your home the way you'd want to: it knows every asset, when each one was last serviced, who services it, what it costs, and — where Home Assistant can measure it — how hard it's actually being used. It runs entirely on your own Home Assistant box. Your data never leaves the house.

Getting started

Open Settings → ＋ Add a home and type your address. KasaKeeper researches the property (listings, photos, aerial imagery) and proposes the systems it detects — pool, aircon, solar, gutters. Tick what's real, and it builds each one's maintenance schedule from sensible defaults.

If your services already have history, tap Start tracking on the dashboard to mark everything serviced today — countdowns begin from there. Or set each task's Last done date by hand for accurate due dates from day one.

The dashboard

  • Health gauge — one score for the whole house, with a verdict line and a segmented bar showing the true proportions of overdue · due soon · on track, plus your committed spend for the next 90 days.
  • Needs attention — every job that wants action now. The ✓ button logs it done (and its cost) in one tap; tapping the row opens the asset.
  • Quotes in flight — chips for every open conversation with a trade. Tap through to manage them.
  • Coming up — the next few weeks on a date rail, with who's doing each job.
  • Keeping watch — weather-aware nudges (wind before gutter cleans, heat before pool care) from your own Home Assistant weather.
  • Something's wrong? — describe any problem in plain words (add a photo if you like); KasaKeeper triages it, tells you what to do right now, and finds the right kind of tradesperson.

Assets

Adding one

The fastest way: ＋ → 📷 Snap. Photograph the appliance's nameplate — KasaKeeper reads the make, model and serial, then automatically researches that exact unit: real specs, the manufacturer's maintenance schedule, and the manual. One tap on ✓ Apply schedule and the asset is fully set up with the maker's own intervals.

Or browse the catalog (＋) and pick a system — each comes with a standard schedule you can tune. Add anything free-form — a limestone wall, a pond, a timber deck — and KasaKeeper researches what that thing actually needs and proposes the right services; one tap applies them.

The asset page

  • Model intelligence — for any asset with a make/model, 🔎 Look up features fetches specs, the maker's schedule and the manual. Nothing changes without your Apply.
  • The manual — once found, tap ⤓ keep a copy and the PDF is stored on your own box: it opens on every device, forever, even if the manufacturer's site dies.
  • Maker's intervals — tasks tuned from the manual are marked maker's interval so you know which numbers are the manufacturer's and which are defaults.
  • Usage tracking — link a Home Assistant entity and jobs come due by actual run-hours or kWh, not just the calendar: "150 h since last service" beats "6 months". The readout shows 42 / 150 hrs · maker's.
  • Warranties — set the date once; the Warranties tab (in Assets) tracks every expiry, soonest first.
  • DIY — jobs you do yourself: tap 🛠 DIY on any task. It stays on the schedule but stops suggesting suppliers and will never auto-email anyone.

Schedule

The triage view: every active job grouped by who's on the hook — Needs a supplier Awaiting a quote Assigned DIY · yours to do — each row carrying exactly one next action: find, manage the quote, book, or call. Editing, snoozing and deleting live on the asset page (tap the row, then the task).

Trades & quotes

🔎 Find a service searches the best-reviewed local businesses for that specific asset — ratings, reviews, photos, websites. From a result you can:

  • Request quote — KasaKeeper drafts the enquiry email; you review and approve every send. Replies are read automatically (read-only) and the quote card fills itself in: amounts, offered dates. Pick a date and the confirmation email is drafted for your approval.
  • Save — keep them as your provider for that asset; every job logged against them builds their history and lifetime spend.

Auto-book (per task, optional): when a job with a linked provider comes due, KasaKeeper emails them for dates from its own mailbox and pings you to confirm. It only ever acts inside business hours, one enquiry at a time, and nothing is booked without you.

Service packs: bought 10 pool cleans up front? Record the pack on the asset and every ✓ Done draws one down.

Ask

The house assistant. Ask anything about your own home — "what needs doing this month?", "who services the aircon and what do they cost a year?", "log the gutter clean as done" — it answers from your real data and can make the edits for you.

Themes & devices

Two looks, one brand: Night (dark, made for the wall tablet) and Paper (warm light, lovely on a desktop). Each device keeps its own choice in Settings → Appearance; "Match system" follows the device's light/dark setting.

KasaKeeper is a PWA sharing one live store: phone, wall tablet and desktop all see the same house, instantly. Add it to your phone's home screen from the browser share menu.

The morning brief

At 8am (your Home Assistant timezone), a push notification summarises what's overdue, what's due soon, and any weather nudges — via your Home Assistant companion app. Toggle it in Settings.

Your data

Everything lives on your own Home Assistant box (/data, included in HA backups). API keys stay server-side and are set in the add-on's Configuration tab — never in the app. The email loop reads your dedicated mailbox read-only, and every outgoing email is approved by you first.
